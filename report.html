Defending human rights where dictators rule — a year of releases, launches, and freedom tech.
“I believe the dictatorship has to end, and the Castro dynasty has to end, as well. Because as long as there is a Castro in power, there will be corruption.” — Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara, Cuban artist and prisoner of conscience, upon his release into exile in July 2026 after five years of imprisonment, following HRF's WGAD petition and advocacy
Dear friends,
In July, after 266 days in a Chinese prison, Pastor Ezra Jin walked free — his case raised directly between presidents because thousands of people refused to let his name be forgotten. That same month, the Cuban artist Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara was forced into exile rather than spend one more day in prison — and told the world what every dictator fears to hear: the dictatorship has to end.
These two men have never met. They were born into different countries, languages, and faiths. Yet they are part of a single story, and it is the story of this report: isolation is the dictator's favorite weapon, and connection is the dissident's answer.
This year we gave that answer a sharper map. In February, HRF launched the Tyranny Tracker, a public classification of 179 countries and territories by the honest names of their governments — democratic, hybrid, or fully authoritarian. Naming tyranny is not pessimism. It is the beginning of strategy, because no government should be able to hide what it is behind what it calls itself.
The instruments of defiance multiplied as well. At our Agent Camps, activists from Venezuela to Iran deployed open-source AI agents that draft, translate, and organize faster than any censorship office can keep up. Through our Financial Freedom program, human rights defenders hold money no dictator can freeze. And when our "Power Plays" podcast carried the authoritarian history of the World Cup to more than 100,000 listeners, sport became an argument that dictators could not control.
In Oslo, under the midnight sun, the Freedom Fellowship grew to 76 leaders across 37 countries, and the Oslo Freedom Forum gathered advocates from 111 countries — journalists and pastors, rappers and lawyers, each one proof that the instinct to be free does not respect borders.
None of this happened because it was easy. It happened because dissidents took risks that most of us cannot imagine, and because people like you refused to look away. HRF is proud to be their instrument. The heroes are the ones who sign their names in defiance, and they appear on nearly every page that follows.
To every dictator reading this: your walls are lower than they look, and your prisoners are your best ambassadors of freedom. To every reader: the isolated are connecting. Join them.
Thor Halvorssen Céline Assaf-Boustani

01
HRF Mission
The Human Rights Foundation (HRF) is a nonpartisan, nonprofit organization that promotes and protects human rights globally, with a focus on closed societies.
HRF believes that all human beings are entitled to freedom of self-determination, freedom from tyranny, the right to speak freely, to associate with those of like mind, and to leave and enter their countries. Individuals in a free society must be accorded equal treatment and due process under the law and must have the opportunity to participate in the governments of their countries; HRF's ideals likewise find expression in the conviction that all human beings have the right to be free from arbitrary detention or exile and from interference and coercion in matters of conscience. HRF does not support nor condone violence.
Strategies
Legal Advocacy & Policy Research
HRF produces legal and policy reports, public letters, and impact litigation briefs to defend prisoners of conscience, bring attention to serious human rights violations, and ultimately influence institutions and policymakers.
Global Advocacy & Campaigns
HRF develops and executes innovative campaigns, using new technologies and creative mediums to advocate for democracy and human rights around the world.
Public Education & Events
HRF popularizes the struggle for freedom and democracy through the Oslo Freedom Forum conference series, global events, podcasts, and other educational efforts, bringing human rights into the mainstream of public discussion.
Direct Support
HRF provides human rights advocates with the resources, skills, contacts, and attention they need to make a difference in their countries.

Legal and policy initiatives form the backbone of HRF's advocacy work.

Legal Advocacy & Policy Research

Impact Litigation

Through its Impact Litigation program, HRF's in-house team of international lawyers advocates on behalf of brave individuals before international and quasi-judicial bodies, including the United Nations Working Group on Arbitrary Detention (WGAD). To date, the WGAD has issued decisions on most of the cases submitted by HRF, with every ruling favoring the client.
HRF represents prisoners of conscience whose cases are emblematic of a dictatorship's brutality. Litigation before the WGAD addresses the inadequacies of judicial systems in authoritarian regimes and offers remedies for pursuing justice for victims of corrupt judicial systems.

Tyranny Tracker

 HRF tracks and assesses fundamental democratic freedoms across countries and territories worldwide. Backed by a broad network of local human rights advocates, HRF's internal research team analyzes the presence of electoral competition, freedom of dissent, and institutional accountability in each nation. Using a distinctive qualitative approach, the team categorizes governments as democratic, hybrid, or fully authoritarian.
 In February 2026, HRF launched the Tyranny Tracker — the first public-facing version of this global classification. At launch, the platform featured 879 country and territory classifications built by 11 research fellows, spanning 179 countries and territories and 42 indicators of freedom.
Democracies: 106
Governments that largely guarantee free and fair elections, the freedom to dissent, and an independent judiciary.
Hybrid Authoritarian Regimes: 17
Regimes that generally allow for a non-trivial degree of electoral competition and basic political freedoms but deny these democratic rights and fundamental freedoms to a degree that they make a change of government through democratic means very difficult.
Fully Authoritarian Regimes: 55
Regimes that systematically deny their citizens meaningful electoral competition, basic political freedoms, and an independent judiciary willing and capable of checking the regime's ability to repress basic democratic freedoms.

Legal Advocacy & Policy Research

Tyranny Tracker

 The Tyranny Tracker quickly became HRF's flagship policy anchor: the team presented it at the National Endowment for Democracy, the University of Oxford, the University of Chicago, Stanford University, and other institutions through the second half of the year. The Washington Post and The Free Press cited it in their own reporting, and an Azerbaijani reader wrote to the team after finding the story of his shuttered media outlet in his country's classification.
 The regime response came swiftly: in October, Russia's communications regulator blocked access to the Tyranny Tracker within Russia — and days later, the Russian government designated HRF itself an "undesirable organization," criminalizing any Russian who engages with HRF's work. The team is now expanding next year's edition with at least eight more country classifications and updated indicators, and is publishing four regional reports on transnational repression, the first focused on authoritarian collaboration, scheduled for October 2026.

Combating Kleptocracy

 HRF's Combating Kleptocracy program exposes how dictatorships use financial secrecy and influence campaigns to entrench their regimes, smother democratic opposition, and enrich themselves that much further. Through research, public awareness campaigns, and media coverage, HRF has taken a global lead in highlighting the threats of kleptocratic regimes worldwide.
The Dissident's Mic
A new interview series on authoritarianism worldwide launched in mid-2026 — featuring Félix Maradiaga on Nicaragua, Tutu Alicante on Equatorial Guinea, and Jewher Ilham on China and the Uyghurs.
Dictators and Diplomas: Why Foreign Autocracies Funding American Universities Threaten Democracy, Destroys Academic Freedom, and Entrenches Anti-Democratic Regimes
This first-ever analysis traces nearly $9 billion in financial flows from authoritarian regimes to leading American universities since 2014. It examines how these gifts, contracts, and joint research centers help dictatorships launder their reputations, influence students and faculty — including those cycling through official positions in the U.S. government — and gain access to sensitive technology, and offers recommendations for universities and regulators.
The CCP's Authoritarian Influence on Latin American Democracy
HRF's report analyzes how the People's Republic of China has entrenched itself in Latin America over the past decade — combining economic opportunity with strategic statecraft across financing, diplomacy, security cooperation, and narrative strategies, including disinformation, while working to isolate Taiwan — and presents recommendations for Latin American democracies and their partners to counter democratic erosion.
Documenting Power Online (Sudan)
HRF's report examines how Sudanese activists have used digital tools to expose and challenge external involvement in Sudan's war. Expected publication: second half of 2026.
2026 pipeline (second half)
Transnational repression regional reports (Middle East and North Africa; Asia Pacific; Europe and Central Asia; Africa), an Asia gender-based violence report, the NBA's relationship with authoritarians, and kleptocracy country reports on Nicaragua, Cameroon, and Serbia.

Wear Your Values

HRF's Wear Your Values (WYV) program aims to shed light on the hidden social costs of the fashion industry. Through creative advocacy campaigns and educational exhibitions, WYV raises awareness of forced labor, promotes transparency in global supply chains, and encourages ethical shopping among consumers.
Those interested in collaborating can contact the program at wearyourvalues@hrf.org.
UN Engagement & Transparency Partnership
In February, program lead Claudia Bennett met the UN Special Rapporteur on contemporary slavery to map collaboration. WYV also launched a year-long partnership with Transparentem, investigating factories to guarantee that merchandise at OFF and beyond is free from forced labor — making HRF the first conference series able to certify ethical supply chains.
San Francisco Community Fashion Week
In March, Bennett spoke at San Francisco Community Fashion Week on how consumers, designers, technologists, and lawyers can eliminate forced labor from fashion.
OFF 2026 Roundtable
Ethical Merch Milestone
The Transparentem partnership moves HRF and OFF toward a published list of verified forced-labor-free merchandise — which would make HRF and OFF the first NGO and conference series able to claim with certainty that they are supporting ethical supply chains and combating tyranny.
During OFF 2026 in June, WYV convened a private roundtable of industry leaders, activists, NGO executives, and survivors of forced labor to define the program's next front.
